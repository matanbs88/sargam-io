# Indian notation converters competitors and search demand

Prepared for the founder on September 30, 2026. This report examines the two supplied converters, competing products, public audience evidence, and implications for Sargam.io. The conclusion is that demand exists, but translation alone is already well served. Our strongest proposed differentiation is a reliable journey from a song to a corrected, printable Indian score and instrument-specific practice.

This is research and a proposed backlog, not an implementation or release record. Public pages were reviewed; competitor conversion accuracy, latency, exports, and paid experiences were not tested. Recommendations below are our analysis, not facts established by competitor marketing.

## What the supplied converters teach us

### Kavitha and Western to Carnatic

The [supplied article](https://www.kavithaanbarasu.com/post/western_to_carnatic) promotes Western-to-Carnatic conversion and links to an Android app. It was published July 17, 2023 and updated February 7, 2025. Although it mentions browser access, this review did not verify a working browser converter there.

The [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.peritia.westerntocarnatic) describes note-entry buttons, input/output text boxes, offline conversion, and copying results for printing. This is notation translation, not evidence of audio recognition.

**Recommended lesson:** provide an immediately understandable conversion surface with paired original and translated notes. Avoid making a user upload audio when they already have readable note text. Explain the chosen Sa alongside the result and offer Listen, Practice, and PDF as the next actions.

### IndiPiano

The [converter](https://indipiano.com/indian-western-notations-convertor/) exposes input notation, output notation, Sa selection, conversion, and download. It lists Hindustani/Carnatic formats, several Indian scripts, and international notation systems. Its examples use `M` for natural Ma and `tM` for sharpened Ma. Our notation dialect must therefore be explicit; case alone is not a universal musical convention.

Its [MIDI importer](https://indipiano.com/midi-to-notations/) advertises track separation, key/time-signature extraction, measures, and notation downloads. These are documented capabilities, not independently benchmarked results.

**Recommended lessons:** make notation language and musical tradition separate settings; preserve a musician's original notation for comparison; and expose MIDI import as a fast path when structured source music already exists. Do not interpret mixed lyrics and notes with unqualified global string replacements. Unknown symbols should survive conversion and produce warnings rather than silently disappearing.

Do not equate Carnatic support with replacing Hindustani syllables. Before claiming it, validate spelling, octave and ornament conventions with a Carnatic teacher and explicit test fixtures. Nor should ordinary chromatic note translation claim to reproduce performance nuances from the source.

## Quantitative demand evidence

The accessible [Semrush NotationsWorld report](https://www.semrush.com/website/notationsworld.com/overview/) covers **January 2026**, updated February 19, not September. It estimates **171,770 visits**; **84.47%** from India. Within its India segment, **80.77%** is mobile. These are modeled session estimates, not owner analytics or unique users.

Its India keyword table gives the following estimated monthly search volumes:

| Query | Estimated monthly searches |
| --- | ---: |
| flute notes | 3,600 |
| krishna flute notes | 1,300 |
| bollywood songs harmonium notes | 880 |
| radha krishna flute notes | 880 |

These four queries are a narrow historical sample, not total market size. Queries can overlap, and one person can search repeatedly. This does **not** establish how many people seek our exact integrated product, nor willingness to pay. Do not project those visits or installs into revenue.

**Interpretation:** there is observable demand for finding playable notes, including devotional/flute and Bollywood/harmonium intentions. The mobile share supports prioritizing mobile score readability and playback reliability. The size of demand specifically for automatic YouTube-to-Sargam remains unquantified.

Google's [Keyword Planner documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575?hl=en) explains that average monthly searches depend on selected location, period and network, and include close variants. [Google Trends](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533?hl=en) normalizes relative interest; its 0–100 index is not a people/search count. No Trends graph was measured in this report.

## Proposed product priorities

1. **Finish the core promise first.** Deliver source-verified complete catalog entries and a measured live audio-transcription path. The latest October 1 checkpoint records 35 playable studies/exercises plus three source-verified complete pieces (38 ready entries, only 3/100 verified complete), and labels the transcription demo as Mock. A converter page must not conceal those gaps.
2. **Add a small free notation converter.** Input format, output format, explicit Sa, paired preview, copy/download, and Open in Practice. Deterministic note conversion should not incur audio-provider calls. Keep the primary home action for YouTube/audio.
3. **Introduce correction of rhythm as well as pitch.** Users need onset, duration, rests, bar structure, and phrase corrections before exporting or trusting transcription. Show ambiguous results explicitly.
4. **Make library entries discoverable individually.** Propose canonical `/songs/[slug]` pages with immediate notation, title/composer/source/version, practice and PDF. These are a recommendation, not an assertion that the current app already exposes them.
5. **Separate reference Sa from audio transposition.** Explain whether a control changes labels, playback pitch, or both. Retain one shared timing model for score, instrument and export.
6. **Expand traditions after validation.** Keep Hindustani and Carnatic semantics distinct from script selection. Maintain piano, harmonium and bansuri rather than pivoting the entire brand to one instrument.

Suggested acceptance fixtures for the converter: accidentals including flats/sharps; tonic other than C; octave marks; `M/tM` versus `m/M` profiles; rests and held notes; two notes inside one beat; bar lines; mixed lyrics; invalid tokens; and round-trip preservation. These tests are proposed, not yet executed against the supplied tools.

## Validation plan and decision

**Recommendation:** compete on trusted score quality and the complete practice loop, not on claiming an empty market or a unique note translator.

Run a small measured pilot with approximately 20 musicians across our three instruments and five teachers. These recruitment numbers are proposed, not completed. Give them verified songs plus their own recordings. Measure time to useful practice, corrections required, playback/export success, repeated sessions, and seven-day return. Ask for payment intent only after they experience the working product.

Publish a first set of individually searchable, complete song pages and measure discovery-to-play, play-to-PDF/save, and return-to-practice. Establish targets from pilot baselines rather than inventing industry conversion rates. Free utility traffic is useful only when it feeds successful practice.

Priority after catalog/core transcription gates: (1) source-and-output comparison with explicit Sa, (2) deterministic text conversion without provider cost, (3) rhythm-aware correction, (4) individually indexed song pages, and (5) teacher-validated tradition-specific notation. Keep conversion, script selection and audible transposition separate. Adoption of these lessons is proposed here; no converter functionality was implemented in this research pass.

Additional discovery during the founder's requested review: [Swara Notebook](https://swaranotebook.com/) advertises Bhatkhande-format editing, playback while typing, and Bangla/English/Devanagari. Its public counter reports 175 bandishes and 33,605 notes across four taals and three languages. This is a self-reported content counter, not a user or traffic count. Search also surfaced [Playsoloist's harmonium page](https://www.playsoloist.com/harmonium), describing on-device transcription and feedback, but the direct page could not be retrieved; treat it as a competitor lead, not independently verified functionality. Fresh public searches for the two supplied domains still yielded no reliable traffic or active-user estimates.

Recommended implementation scope from these findings: a separate deterministic text converter, not a homepage pivot. Explicit input/output dialects and Sa; original/translated side-by-side preview; preserved octaves, bar lines, beat grouping and unknown text; copy, PDF and Open in Practice. Validate with fixtures before claiming Carnatic support. This refresh changes research and priorities only; no product functionality was implemented by this report.

Reopened both supplied pages, the linked Google Play listing, SRGM.io, Swarakosh, Bollypiano, and the primary Semrush NotationsWorld report. The converter app still displays **1K+ downloads**. The accessible Semrush report still refers to **January 2026**, not current-month traffic: 171,770 estimated visits and the keyword volumes above. Fresh domain-specific searches did not establish monthly traffic or active users for either supplied website. Conflicting, undated traffic figures from secondary aggregators were excluded rather than combined into a misleading range. No competitor conversion accuracy or sound quality benchmark was performed during this refresh.

Implementation decision: retain YouTube/audio transcription as the primary intake; propose a separate deterministic text-conversion utility with explicit Sa, notation-dialect selection and preserved rhythm/octaves. This research does not authorize replacing the selected Mehfil design or asserting that Carnatic performance nuances can be recovered by a chromatic lookup table.

Confidence is high for what public pages and install counters display, moderate for third-party historical estimates, and unresolved for competitor accuracy, active users and payment conversion. The report deliberately excludes undated secondary traffic aggregates, search-result counts, spam comments and unsupported total-market numbers.
