# Sargam.io launch strategy

## Decision

Launch first as a focused waitlist landing experience with a working,
clearly-labelled interactive practice demo. Do **not** launch as a promise
that any YouTube link is already transcribed: live ingestion, provider terms,
rights, accounts, payment, and persistence are not ready.

The first promise is:

> Bring a melody into your Sa. See it, play it, and practice it your way.

The landing experience must make the future product tangible without claiming
the mock demo is a live transcription service.

## Initial wedge

Target English/Hinglish-speaking Indian learners who already search for simple
relative notes for keyboard, harmonium, and Bansuri. The immediate pain is not
"I need another course"; it is "I know a melody and need it in my Sa, in a
notation and visual practice form I can actually use."

Start with one audience message and validate it before broadening:

- **Primary:** melody learners on keyboard/harmonium who use Sargam or letter
  notes and share short practice videos.
- **Design partner:** Bansuri learners and teachers who can validate the
  instrument-specific learning language, but must not be promised universal
  fingering correctness.

The destination remains a full Indian-music practice platform. The launch
wedge is transcription-to-practice, not a generic course catalogue.

## Landing page structure

1. **Hero:** heritage-led headline, one visual of the real performance canvas,
   and a single CTA: `Join the founding waitlist`.
2. **Interactive proof:** a short, labelled demo phrase where visitors choose
   Sa, notation, Keyboard or Bansuri, and watch the timed practice canvas.
3. **Three outcomes:** `Choose your Sa`, `See relative Sargam`, `Practice on
   your instrument`.
4. **Founder intake:** email, instrument, learning level, musical style,
   country/city, and the one song or practice problem they most want solved.
5. **Trust:** state exactly what is not live yet; offer a response timeframe
   only when the team can honor it. Collect explicit marketing consent.
6. **Early access:** explain that waitlist position improves through a useful
   referral or a short learner interview, not through empty gamification.

The page should have analytics events for CTA click, demo interaction,
waitlist start, completed signup, selected instrument, and requested song. No
audio recording, song URL, or personal data beyond the stated form should be
collected before privacy terms and retention policy exist.

## Seed content and traffic

Yes: seed a small catalogue. No: do not publish unlicensed Bollywood or Western
song transcriptions merely because they are search-friendly.

Use 12–20 **rights-cleared showcase sessions** before launch:

1. Original short melodies commissioned from or contributed by a creator under
   a written product/marketing licence.
2. Traditional/public-domain material only after a jurisdiction-specific rights
   check; a traditional melody can still have a protected arrangement or
   recording.
3. Teacher-authored alankars, sargam patterns, Taal exercises, and original
   phrase studies, each with attribution and permission.
4. Creator-approved demonstrations built from their own original composition.

Each session gets a high-quality page and one vertical performance video. The
SEO target is the *practice problem* (for example, `practice Sargam on
harmonium` or `Bansuri Sa Re Ga visual practice`) rather than claiming a
database of copyrighted song notes. Add song-title pages only after counsel and
the rights holder/provider position are clear.

## 90-day validation plan

### Days 0–21: prove the message

- Ship the waitlist page, privacy copy, event analytics, and a small demo.
- Recruit 20 interviews: 10 keyboard/harmonium learners, 5 Bansuri learners,
  and 5 teachers/creators.
- Publish 3 creator-quality demo clips using cleared material.
- Success signal: at least 10% visitor-to-waitlist conversion from warm,
  relevant traffic and 10 people willing to schedule an interview or submit a
  real practice request.

### Days 22–50: prove repeatable demand

- Add the first 12–20 cleared showcase sessions.
- Test three messages: `your Sa`, `visual instrument practice`, and
  `Sargam for songs you love` (without claiming unavailable functionality).
- Partner with 3–5 micro-creators/teachers and give them a branded shareable
  demo rather than paid broad reach.
- Success signal: 100 qualified waitlist members, 25% or more returning to
  interact with the demo, and a clear top instrument/persona.

### Days 51–90: prove paid problem and operating viability

- Select one legal ingestion/provider path and build a private alpha, not a
  public broad launch.
- Invite 25–50 waitlist members with diverse keys/instruments.
- Measure time to first useful session, repeat practice, correction needs, and
  willingness to pay—not vanity signups.
- Do not charge until source policy, credits, data handling, and failure/refund
  behaviour are implemented.

## What must happen before public alpha

1. Resolve YouTube/audio ingestion permission and provider terms in writing.
2. Add authentication, server-owned credits, consent, privacy, abuse control,
   and durable session storage.
3. Test a live transcription provider on a benchmark set with timing and
   confidence results; retain a human correction path.
4. Have a qualified Indian-music practitioner review the public notation,
   Devanagari, Taal, and each instrument claim.
5. Maintain a rights ledger for every public audio, loop, sample, visual, and
   showcase arrangement.

## Competitive evidence

Riyaz publicly markets guided practice, vocal monitoring, large lesson
libraries, user song uploads, progress tracking, and real-time feedback.
RiyazStudio emphasizes real-player accompaniment and configurable practice
tools. SurSadhak packages Tabla, Tanpura, tuning, recording, notation and a
community. These validate demand, but Sargam.io should differentiate through a
modern web canvas, Sa-first relative notation, instrument-specific visual
practice, and creator-ready presentation—not by copying their content or
claims.
